Best Hookah Flavors to Try: A Beginner's Guide

Published August 5, 2026 · 6 min read

Walking up to a hookah menu for the first time can feel overwhelming. Most lounges carry dozens of flavors across multiple brands, and the names alone don't always tell you what you're getting into. Grape Mint sounds straightforward enough. But what about Code69? Or Irish Kiss?

The good news is that hookah flavors are more forgiving than you might think. Unlike, say, ordering a whiskey neat when you've never had liquor, there's no "wrong" first choice. Some flavors are just better starting points than others. This guide breaks down the most popular options, how to mix them, and which ones to try once you're ready for something bolder.

What Makes a Good Hookah Flavor

Before getting into specific flavors, it helps to understand what separates a great hookah session from a mediocre one. The flavor itself matters, but three other factors play just as big a role.

Tobacco quality. Premium brands like Al Fakher and Starbuzz use higher-grade tobacco leaf and better flavor extracts. The difference between a premium brand and a gas-station-quality shisha is night and day. Cheap tobacco burns harsh and loses flavor within 20 minutes. Premium brands deliver smooth, consistent flavor for 45 minutes or longer.

Heat management. This is the staff's job at a good lounge. Too much heat burns the tobacco and gives you a harsh, acrid taste. Too little and you get thin, wispy smoke with almost no flavor. At Star Light Cafe, every bowl is packed by hand and monitored throughout your session. When the coals need rotating or replacing, the team handles it without you having to ask.

Freshness. Shisha tobacco dries out if it sits too long. A lounge that moves through inventory quickly, like one with a steady regular crowd, will always have fresher product. You can actually tell: fresh shisha is moist and sticky, while old product feels dry and crumbly.

Top Beginner-Friendly Flavors

These five flavors are popular for a reason. They're smooth, easy to inhale, and give you a clear sense of what hookah is supposed to taste like without anything too polarizing.

Mint

The most universally liked flavor in hookah. Clean, cooling, and refreshing. Mint is impossible to dislike, and it pairs well with almost anything. If you're smoking with a group and can't agree on a flavor, Mint is the safe pick. Both Al Fakher and Starbuzz make strong versions.

Double Apple

The classic Middle Eastern hookah flavor. Despite the name, it doesn't taste like a Fuji apple. Double Apple has a distinct anise and licorice character, sweet and herbal. It's the flavor most traditional hookah smokers grew up on, and it produces thick, aromatic clouds. If you enjoy licorice candy or fennel, you'll love it. If not, skip to the next one.

Watermelon

Light, sweet, and summery. Watermelon is as easy as hookah gets. No sharp edges, no polarizing aftertaste. It's a great first flavor because it gives you a clear taste profile without being overwhelming. Al Fakher's Watermelon is a go-to at most lounges.

White Grape

Smooth and slightly sweet with a clean finish. White Grape doesn't hit you over the head with flavor. Instead, it builds a mellow, fruity taste that works well for longer sessions. A solid pick if you want something subtle rather than bold.

Blue Mist (Starbuzz)

One of Starbuzz's signature blends. Blue Mist combines blueberry with a cooling mint finish. It's one of the most popular premium flavors in the hookah world, and for good reason. The fruit-mint combo hits a sweet spot that appeals to almost everyone. It's a step up in complexity from straight Mint or Watermelon, but still very approachable. How to Mix Flavors Like a Pro

Once you've tried a few individual flavors, mixing is where hookah gets really interesting. Most lounges, including ours, are happy to mix two flavors in the same bowl. Here are some proven combinations.

  • Mint + White Grape: The cooling mint cuts through the sweetness. A crowd favorite.
  • Watermelon + Mint: Summer in a bowl. Light, refreshing, long-lasting.
  • Double Apple + Mint: The traditional pairing. The mint smooths out the anise notes.
  • Blue Mist + Lemon: Tangy and cool. Great if you like citrus.
  • Irish Kiss + Peach (Starbuzz + Al Fakher): Dessert-like richness. The coffee-chocolate notes meet fruity sweetness.

A general rule for mixing: pair a fruit flavor with mint or cream. Avoid mixing two strong, competing fruits (like Mango and Strawberry) unless you've tried both individually and know they won't clash.

Bold Flavors for the Adventurous

Once you've dialed in the basics, these options are worth exploring. They have bigger, more complex flavor profiles that experienced smokers tend to gravitate toward.

Sex on the Beach (Starbuzz)

A tropical cocktail-inspired blend with peach, orange, and cranberry notes. It's sweet without being cloying, and the layered fruit profile keeps things interesting throughout a full session. One of Starbuzz's best sellers.

Code69 (Starbuzz)

The name is playful, the flavor is serious. It's a mystery blend, meaning Starbuzz doesn't officially list the ingredients. Most people describe it as a tropical, slightly citrus-forward mix. It's become a cult favorite precisely because of the mystery.

Irish Kiss (Starbuzz)

Rich and creamy with subtle coffee and chocolate undertones. Irish Kiss is for the smoker who wants something warm and dessert-like rather than fruity. It pairs surprisingly well with a cup of Moroccan mint tea, which you can order at our lounge.

Flavor choice matters, but preparation is what separates a good session from a great one. Here's what goes into every hookah at Star Light Cafe.

Every bowl is hand-packed with the right density. Too tight and the airflow restricts. Too loose and the flavor dissipates. Our staff packs each bowl based on the specific brand and flavor, because Al Fakher and Starbuzz behave differently under heat.

We use natural coconut charcoal, not the quick-light chemical discs that some lounges cut corners with. Natural coals burn cleaner, last longer, and don't add a chemical aftertaste to your smoke. The difference is significant.

Throughout your session, the team monitors your hookah. Coals get rotated every 15 to 20 minutes to maintain even heat distribution. If the flavor starts to thin out, they'll replace the coals or adjust the setup. You shouldn't have to wave someone down for basic maintenance.
